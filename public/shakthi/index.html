This is a Nadappu

A quiet, deeply personal journey across Kodagu.

On the 26th of September we set out on foot — about 35 kilometres, some ten hours — from Talakaveri, the sacred birthplace of Mother Kaveri. We walk through Bhagamandala, Aiyangeri, Nelji and Kakkabe, and complete the journey at Padi Igguthappa, sacred to our land, our rain, our harvest and our sustenance.

Not a race Not a tourism event Not a spectacle

It is a walk of gratitude, remembrance and responsibility — a way to reconnect with the land that sustains us, to listen to our elders, to honour what we have inherited, and to ask what we will leave behind.

“We walk not to claim Kodagu. We walk to remember our duty to her.

At the head of the walk

The Gejje Thande leads us.

The ceremonial staff carries the spirit of the journey — a reminder that every step is guided by our traditions, the wisdom of our elders, and a deep respect for the sacred land and places through which we walk. It goes at the head of the Nadappu. We follow.

Before you walk

Come prepared. Walk at your own pace.

Late September is the tail of the monsoon — expect rain, mist, mud and warm, humid days. Water and support points are placed along the route, and vehicles accompany the walk for anyone who needs to rest or step out.

  • Sturdy, broken-in shoes
  • A light raincoat or poncho
  • A refillable water bottle
  • A hat & sunscreen
  • Any personal medication
  • A small snack

A word on safety

This is a strenuous walk over long hours and hilly terrain. Please register your health honestly, walk within your limits, and follow the guidance of the organisers and the support team on the day. Children and elders are warmly welcome for the shorter stretches and the completion — at their own pace, with support.

Questions

Before you set out

Is there a fee?

No. Registration is free — but it is required, and places are limited to 108 walkers this year.

Can I walk only part of the way?

Yes. Join for 1, 5 or 10 km, or the full ~35 km. And everyone is invited to the final steps into Padi Igguthappa around 6:00 PM — the completion we share as one community.

What if it rains?

We walk. Late September is the tail of the monsoon, so come with a raincoat and shoes that handle mud. We'll only change plans for genuine safety reasons.

Can elders and children take part?

Warmly, yes — for the shorter stretches and the completion, at their own pace and with support. Please register their health details honestly.

How do I get back afterwards?

Transport and pickup arrangements will be shared with registered walkers closer to the day.

Is this a protest or a claim over land?

No. It is a Nadappu — a walk of gratitude and responsibility. We walk not to claim Kodagu, but to remember our duty to her.
